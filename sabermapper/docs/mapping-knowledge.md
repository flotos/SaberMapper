# Technical mapping knowledge and review taxonomy

Retrieved and source-checked 2026-09-22. These are contextual review prompts for all five Standard difficulties. They guide inspection rather than automate musical or playability judgments.

| ID and kind | Contextual note, example and counterexample | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| K1 timing, mechanical | Check that waveform accents align with the beat grid at the start, middle, and end. A consistent half-beat kick on a checked grid can be intentional; an entire song requiring 1/32-beat manual corrections suggests bad audio/BPM alignment. | [BSMG Basic Mapping, Timing & Rhythm](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/basic-mapping.html#timing-rhythm) | High for need to check, low for automatic detection of intent |
| K2 emphasis, heuristic | Let a larger sound earn a larger physical hit. A chorus crash may justify a double after verse singles; doubles on every quiet eighth note erase contrast. Maintain recognizable representation when a sound returns. | [BSMG Basic Mapping, Emphasis & Consistency](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/basic-mapping.html#emphasis-consistency) | High as editorial heuristic |
| K3 parity and setup, technical | Track each hand through entry, phrase, and exit. Repeated same-direction cuts at rapid spacing merit review; a deliberate reset after a rest or bomb cue can work. A parity flag alone cannot condemn a complex pattern because starting posture, rotation and time matter. | [BSMG Intermediate Mapping, Parity](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/intermediate-mapping.html#parity), [BSMG Basic Mapping, Resets](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/basic-mapping.html#resets) | High for review priority, medium for any automated verdict |
| K4 recurrence, artistic | Repeated song material should be recognizable in mapping vocabulary while permitting a changed exit, emphasis or density. Copying an identical phrase through a newly layered chorus is dull; changing every return arbitrarily loses musical structure. | [BSMG Intermediate Mapping, Consistency](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/intermediate-mapping.html#consistency) | High as design guidance |
| K5 visibility and jump settings, playability | Check center-lane vision obstruction and dense clutter in preview. Adjust NJS and reaction time for the actual density and movement; increasing NJS merely to hide clutter can make timing stricter. An intentional face note with clear surrounding space differs from a wall of center notes. | [BSMG Basic Mapping, Vision Blocks](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/basic-mapping.html#vision-blocks), [BSMG Intermediate Mapping, Note Jump Speed](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/intermediate-mapping.html#note-jump-speed) | High for issue existence, medium for suitable settings |
| K6 lighting, format and accessibility | Basic static events can keep the lane visible and mark sections without a rich lightshow. A flash on a structural accent may help; heavy flashing on every subdivision or darkness before bombs can hinder reading. | [BSMG Basic Lighting](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/basic-lighting.html#lighting-events), [BSMG Basic Mapping, Block Types](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/basic-mapping.html#block-types) | High for basic event behavior |
| K7 obstacles, mechanical | Standard wall shapes have broad editor compatibility; place walls where the player has a readable dodge and where note paths remain possible. An intentional dodge phrase differs from a wall covering all lanes. | [BSMG Basic Mapping, Block Types](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/basic-mapping.html#block-types) | Medium; detailed wall model deferred |
| K8 tempo changes, mechanical | Check note time before and after each native BPM event. A note at the same beat interval after a slowdown lands later in seconds; treating the entire chart as one BPM hides timing drift. | [BSMG Beatmap, BPM Events](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/map-format/beatmap.html) | High for native v2/v3 format; editor custom tempo remains separate |
| K9 grid precision, musical | Use subdivisions that represent heard attacks, and preserve an intentional swung or delayed feel. Moving every slightly off-grid note onto a mechanical 1/4 beat can erase the song's rhythm. | [BSMG Basic Mapping, Timing and Rhythm](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/basic-mapping.html) | High as listening workflow, low for automatic intent |
| K10 hand lead, technical | Review which hand enters a phrase and where it exits. A phrase copied with a changed lead-in may require a different opening direction even if its interior notes were good. | [BSMG Intermediate Mapping, Parity](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/intermediate-mapping.html) | Medium; movement model posture is a hypothesis |
| K11 resets, technical | Give an intentional reset time and a cue. A rest or bomb line may make it legible; a surprise same-direction cut at high speed needs closer playtest. | [BSMG Basic Mapping, Resets](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/basic-mapping.html) | Medium; timing alone does not establish comfort |
| K12 unusual parity, technical | Palm-up and other technical rotations can preserve a coherent phrase. Inspect the preceding posture before declaring an up-cut forehand or a down-cut backhand an error. | [BSMG Intermediate Mapping, Parity](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/intermediate-mapping.html) | Medium; requires experienced human review |
| K13 simultaneous hits, mechanical | Notes intended as one hand's chord should share a readable cut gesture. Same-time notes with incompatible directions deserve inspection even when they occupy different cells. | [ScoreSaber Mapping Criteria, Multinote Hits](https://wiki.scoresaber.com/ranking/criteria/mapping-criteria.html) | High for review question; ranking rules are a separate target |
| K14 crossover, technical | A note across the body may be expressive when it has setup and recovery. Repeating crossovers without room to unwind can accumulate awkward motion. | [BSMG Intermediate Mapping, Parity](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/intermediate-mapping.html) | Low for grid-only strain proxy |
| K15 burst density, pacing | Count short high-rate runs separately from whole-song average density. A short chorus burst can fit the music; sustaining the same demand through a quiet verse changes its character. | [BSMG Intermediate Mapping, Speed and Rhythm](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/intermediate-mapping.html) | Medium as pacing heuristic |
| K16 hit type, emphasis | Choose singles, sliders, chains, stacks and larger shapes to match sound strength and duration. A sustained vocal may suit an arc; a sharp accent may call for a brief hit. | [BSMG Intermediate Mapping, Hit Types](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/intermediate-mapping.html) | Medium; sound interpretation remains subjective |
| K17 arcs and chains, format | Check head/tail order, cells, and connection to nearby notes in the chosen v3 format. A rendered curve may change scoring and the inferred hand route. | [BSMG Beatmap, Arcs and Chains](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/map-format/beatmap.html) | High for object fields, low for feel |
| K18 obstacle visibility, readability | Review whether a wall hides an upcoming note or bomb during its approach. An edge wall outside the note grid can be decorative; treat its effect as context-dependent. | [BSMG Basic Mapping, Vision Blocks](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/basic-mapping.html), [BSMG Beatmap, Obstacles](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/map-format/beatmap.html) | Medium; current checker sees only local beat/cell context |
| K19 reaction time, jump settings | Inspect BPM, NJS and spawn offset together. More jump speed can separate clutter while tightening hit timing; the player's actual reaction time also depends on the game's jump calculation. | [BSMG Intermediate Mapping, Jump Settings](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/intermediate-mapping.html) | High for dependency, low for local proxy accuracy |
| K20 lighting contrast, accessibility | Keep interactive objects visible through dark or flashing passages. Flash patterns can support accents, but their comfort and visibility require visual review in the chosen environment. | [BSMG Basic Lighting, Events](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/basic-lighting.html), [BSMG Intermediate Lighting](https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/intermediate-lighting.html) | Medium; no automated photosensitivity verdict |

The taxonomy below separates what can be checked from what requires a player. [ScoreSaber ranking criteria](https://wiki.scoresaber.com/ranking/criteria) are a competitive ruleset, not the target style for this project.

| Family | Notes | Current evidence | Gap to review |
|---|---|---|---|
| Timing and structure | K1, K8, K9 | Valid fields, rational beats, native tempo conversion, overlap diagnostics | Human-confirmed audio phase, swung feel, bar anchor |
| Musical representation | K2, K4, K15, K16 | Section intents and literal/motif authoring; burst count proxy | Source-layer annotations, repeat interpretation, preference labels |
| Movement | K3, K10–K14 | Movement model 1.1 posture candidates, rate, angle, crossover and reset warnings | Calibrated tech phrases, player-specific comfort, bomb/wall/arc motion |
| Readability and jumps | K5, K18, K19 | NJS/offset reaction proxy and contextual object warnings | Editor/game sightline and jump-distance verification |
| Environment and effects | K6, K7, K17, K20 | v3 objects and basic lights exported; fields checked | In-game object visibility, lighting taste and accessibility review |

Review rubric: record a specific beat range, observed issue, evidence, severity, whether complexity was intentional, and a proposed revision. Mark invalid fields, overlapping cells, or wrong audio alignment as defects. Mark parity, rotation, density, repetition, vision and NJS concerns as review hypotheses until the phrase is seen and played in context. Ask whether the technical motion is readable, recoverable, varied with the song, and enjoyable for this player. Compare before/after after one scoped edit.

Open questions: preferred rotation intensity and endurance for this player; reliable bar/downbeat detection; richer wall and bomb motion guidance; specific editor/game build behavior; whether basic lighting is visibly adequate. These 20 notes establish coverage, not calibration. The source set is mainly BSMG plus ScoreSaber criteria; technical mapping styles and accessibility perspectives need broader review during human playtests.
