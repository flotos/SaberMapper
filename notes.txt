- Rules update: Held singing notes, or intense singing should always be the focus of track.
  Use held note (quite "recently" added in beat saber) for these, up to bottom or bottom to up to match the pitch change if so. For example Living a Lie at 4:24, then 4:28, 4:30

- Rules update: In passages with low musical intensity and little rhythm, place notes
  mostly on note change (pitch or chord change), not on every amplitude peak.
  Examples: Living a Lie intro (0:00-0:28) and the quiet breakdown (~2:38-2:58).
  Detect these passages from evidence, not by hand: no strong drum onsets, and mix
  energy under about half the song median. Both examples score zero on the drum term.
  Inside them:
    - Do not place from energy_rise events on sustained stems. On a held pad or voice
      that detector fires ~6/s (about 3 per beat at 122 BPM) on envelope wobble, not
      on attacks. This is what made the Living a Lie intro read as out of sync - the
      notes faithfully tracked a bad onset list.
    - Keep genuine attacks. The intro piano hits land within ~20 ms of the grid and
      are real musical events.
    - Pick the lead by rhythmic salience, not loudness. The intro pad is louder than
      the piano that actually carries the pulse, so energy-based lead selection missed it.
    - Quantize to 1/2 or whole beat, not 1/4. Sung note changes do not land on 16ths,
      and fine quantization pushes them into gaps.
    - Check section boundaries for an accent sitting on the seam. The loudest attack
      in the Living a Lie intro (beat 32.0) fell between two sections and got no note.
  Pairs with the held-note rule above: on a sustained vocal, the pitch move is both the
  placement trigger and the up/down direction cue.
  Note: the global grid was not the problem - 122 BPM at 0.302 s offset tracks the drums
  to a 7.7 ms median across the whole song.
