# Author or revise a map

In this repository, read workspace-root `PLAYER.md` and the app workspace's `player-profile.json` before choosing difficulty or patterns. Use the documented player's calibration by default, unless the user specifies another player. Expert/ExpertPlus are output labels, not skill estimates. New explicit preferences and actual playtest feedback take priority over historical scores. State the reference-based target briefly; do not claim a generated map has an official star rating. A project can hold several difficulties. Each one declares the player star tier it aims for in `difficulty.target_tier`. Read [difficulties and star tiers](references/difficulties.md) before adding, authoring or revising a difficulty. When this skill is used outside this repository, use the supplied player profile instead.

Read the available song report, exact audio hash, phrase examples with provenance, player preferences, and any existing arrangement. The app does not call an LLM; the assistant authors JSON using its own tools. A user-checked report is useful evidence, not a prerequisite for starting or completing a provisional map.

## Audio first

Standing user rule (2026-09-22): "the audio should ALWAYS be the focus of the work; the goal is mapping audio to notes, not applying random notes." Every note must sit on a sound you can name, such as a drum hit, a sung onset, a pitch change or a riff attack in `musical/RUN/report.json`. Every stretch where the song is playing must be mapped. A section label, role or position (intro, outro, "quiet") never decides the density; the evidence for each bar does. Do not batch-generate maps from coarse section roles. Author each song against its own stems, then run `project check ID --workspace workspace`, which uses the newest evidence run automatically, and resolve every audio finding:

- `audio_unmapped`: the song plays with strong onsets, but the map has at most one note per 4 s. A stretch of 8 s or more blocks `project save`; map it. A shorter one is a warning to fix or justify.
- `note_without_audio` / `low_audio_support`: notes with no onset or pitch change under them. Move them onto the nearest real attack or delete them.
- `audio_evidence_missing`: nothing was checked. Run `music analyze ID --workspace workspace` before judging or delivering the map.
- `vocal_line_unmapped` / `drum_rhythm_unmapped`: the notes miss the salient layer of the bar. Articulated singing leads whenever it is present; while the voice holds or rests, the drum pattern leads and the held vocal rides as an arc. See "Salience: who leads, bar by bar" in [musical focus and evidence](references/musical-focus.md).
- `melody_unmapped`: no voice, drum pattern or declared lead carries the bars, so the pitched line (a pad, choir, synth or legato voice) leads, yet most of its `melody_change` events carry no note. Put a note on each pitch change (the whole, half or quarter beat within 0.13 beat of it) and let the row follow the pitch contour. See "Follow the melody" in [musical focus and evidence](references/musical-focus.md). Living a Lie's intro (2026-09-23) was mapped on beat-grid chord guesses while its choir line changed pitch almost every beat.
- `drum_entry_unmapped`: the drums arrive after 4 s or more away, and fewer than half their hits in the next 4 beats carry a note. Let the focus move to the drums for that bar, then hand it back. See "Focus moves; the whole band weighs in" in [musical focus and evidence](references/musical-focus.md).
- `ensemble_unmapped`: over 16 beats, the notes follow the lead alone and skip every heavy hit of the rest of the band (fewer than 20% of the other stems' strongest accents carry a note). Add the heaviest few that the message names, so the map carries the whole song's weight. A note on a bar's heaviest ensemble accent does not count as filler for `lead_rhythm_diluted`.
- `focus_on_quiet_stem`: a `musical_focus` phrase weights a stem that sits at least 20 dB below its own usual level there. Its events are separator bleed (vocals in an instrumental intro), or the instrument was routed to another stem (Demucs `htdemucs_6s` often puts a soft solo piano in `other`). Follow the most active stem the message names.
- `difficulty_exceeds_intensity` / `intensity_underplayed`: difficulty does not follow loudness. A soft bar asks as much swing demand as the heavy passages, or a heavy run plays easier than the soft ones. Heavier, louder audio must play harder. See "Heavier plays harder" in [musical focus and evidence](references/musical-focus.md).

These warnings also appear in `project get` and `project save` diagnostics. They never block a save, so read them in `project check` and resolve or justify each one before delivering. Each carries `suggestions`: the concrete edit that addresses it (add the draft's notes on the named sounds, retime or remove a note, thin a passage, set new focus weights). Apply them with judgment; they come from the same rules as the rhythm draft.

Draft the rhythm from the audio, not from a grid. `python -m sabermapper music rhythm ID --workspace workspace --propose [--start A --end B] --draft DRAFT.json` proposes note times bar by bar from the critique's own rules. It reads each bar's role from the evidence (soft, riff or sung) and drafts it the way the musical rules in [musical focus and evidence](references/musical-focus.md) describe: riffs as the riff's attacks with the kick on the sixteenth grid but not between chugs, syllables first in sung bars with the band in the voice's gaps and under held arcs, soft bars on melody and chord changes half a beat apart, arcs for held and intense singing, and doubles on a loud bar's heaviest accents. Bar density follows loudness and the target tier. Name a hold the player asked for with `--held SECONDS`, or record it in `player-profile.json` under `held_vocals` (`{"project": ID, "seconds": S}`). Each time names the sound under it. The draft is placed and checked until it raises no `note_without_audio`, `density_exceeds_audio`, `lead_rhythm_*`, intensity or `one_hand_burst` finding, and `remaining` lists what could not be resolved. It is a starting point: edit it musically (keep, drop or add times, pin a deliberate cut), then save it. Scale density with the song's intensity: a soft intro or bridge carried by one instrument takes its strongest attacks, not every attack. Map speech or sparse singing on its syllables, not an even stream. `lead_rhythm_diluted` and `lead_rhythm_unmapped` mean the notes do not follow the bar's lead instrument; see "Follow the lead instrument's rhythm" in [musical focus and evidence](references/musical-focus.md).

## See the song before and after mapping it

You cannot listen, so look. Spectrograms are part of every authoring and review pass:

1. **Separate with the ensemble backend.** `python -m sabermapper music analyze ID --workspace workspace --backend ensemble` finds `.venv-separation` and CUDA by itself. It writes `musical/RUN/overview.png` and returns its path as `overview_image`.
2. **Read the overview before planning sections.** It shows every stem across the whole song: where drums drop out and return, where the voice sings, which stem carries each passage, and where a stem is only bleed (a dark panel).
3. **Render each passage before authoring it.** `python -m sabermapper music spectrogram ID --workspace workspace --start A --end B` writes a PNG of at most about 32 beats, with bar and beat lines, sections, each bar's lead, the current notes, strong attacks (white ticks), pitch, chord and melody changes (cyan ticks), and stem entries (green). Read the image beside `music rhythm` output.
4. **Render it again after saving.** Check that notes sit on the bright attacks of each bar's lead, that entries and drops get a note, and that the heaviest hits of the other stems appear now and then.

Read the image with your file-reading tool. Do not open the studio or ArcViewer for this. Omit `--start` and `--end` for the whole song with the current notes.

## Establish timing from available audio

Work from the application directory (`sabermapper/` in this repository); on Windows use `.venv/Scripts/python` in place of `python`. Locate the requested track in existing projects (`python -m sabermapper project list --workspace workspace`, then `project get ID --workspace workspace`), the local tracklist and workspace audio before asking the user for files or timing. Preserve original audio and source provenance. Use `import-audio --help` for a new project and `python -m sabermapper analyze TRACK --output REPORT.json` for an analysis report; BPM and offset are optional estimates, not information the user must supply. The implementation is in `sabermapper/audio.py` and `sabermapper/timing.py`.

Use onset, energy, tempo alternatives and recurrence candidates to investigate BPM, beat-zero phase and phrase boundaries. Compare plausible half/double tempos and inspect grid-to-onset alignment in separated passages near the start, middle and end; check phase consistency and cumulative drift, not just the global estimator score. Rerun analysis with `--bpm BPM --offset SECONDS` when testing a hypothesis. Supplied BPM produces the report status `reviewed_bpm`; that label alone does not establish human review. If the report is inconclusive, inspect the decoded signal or local analysis helpers before asking the user. Derive provisional section labels from evidence and distinguish inference from listening.

Record the selected timing, exact audio hash, passages checked, method and remaining uncertainty in the project notes or a companion evidence artifact. The analyzer does not establish meter or a bar/downbeat anchor; document any inference separately from its beat-phase estimate. Automated agreement can justify drafting and a provisional export without human timing confirmation. Report fields such as `manual_review_required` describe outstanding human review; do not treat them as blanket permission gates. Preserve human-review flags such as `timing_reviewed` and `playtested` as false until that review actually occurs. Keep a section `resolved: false` while a material timing or authoring ambiguity remains; set it true when the available evidence supports the chosen arrangement, without implying that it was human-reviewed. Never clear it merely to bypass compilation. Ask one specific question only when a blocking ambiguity remains after available analysis, explaining the competing choices and affected passage.

## Author, save and export

For real-song composition or revisions to musical connection, read
[musical focus and evidence](references/musical-focus.md). Use the
local `music` tools to gather complementary evidence, then author phrase focus,
rhythm and movement yourself. The application never delegates composition to an
internal model or converts detector peaks to patterns. Prefer musical events and
rests over stock-pattern density when deciding the rhythm of a phrase.

Read [held notes](references/held-notes.md) before authoring. Held or intense
vocals are the focus of their window and get arcs per that page, with the color
notes that connect them at the head and tail. In low-intensity passages follow
the quiet-passage policy in [musical focus and evidence](references/musical-focus.md).

Read [arrangement contract](references/arrangement.md) before writing. Give each section a musical intent and stable IDs. Use motifs for recognizable recurring phrases and literal notes for a deliberate local change. Consider entry posture, intended motion, exit and recovery. Do not flatten unusual rotations or repetition merely because a heuristic warned; inspect context and ask how the player experienced it. Keep target source-map note arrays hidden until the authored map is frozen for comparison.

For a requested revision, inspect the current file and exact feedback artifact first. Change only the requested unlocked section or shared motif when the requested scope warrants it. Preserve other IDs and `locked: true` sections. For projects, save with `python -m sabermapper project save ID --workspace workspace --revision CURRENT_SHA --arrangement EDITED.json [--difficulty NAME]`, rather than overwriting stored project files. Pass `--difficulty` for any non-primary difficulty on every project command. If the revision conflicts, reread and reconcile with the current content rather than replacing it with an old draft. For standalone arrangements, save a new artifact or show a clear diff.

Author the musical layer and let the placer build the mechanical one. A note needs only `id` and `beat`. `color`, `direction`, `x` and `y` are optional pins that placement never changes; `project save` and `compile` fill the rest so every movement rule holds, and record what they chose in the note's `placed` list. Pin a field only for a deliberate choice, such as a cut that follows a gesture in the music or a cell that traces a pitch contour. On a later save, placed values stay unless your rhythm edit makes them break a rule. Editing a placed value pins it, and deleting a field asks the placer to choose again. Arcs and chains stay yours: their head and tail notes take the arc's hand, cut and cell, and the placer keeps every other sound off the held saber (standing rule after End of You 0:42; see [held notes](references/held-notes.md)). The rules the placer keeps are standing player rules. Swing flow was set 2026-09-22 after reports at The Revival 1:03 and Living a Lie 1:38, and tightened on 2026-09-23 after Borrowed Waters: every cut starts where the same hand's previous cut left the saber. Consecutive same-hand cuts alternate forehand/backhand and turn at least 90 degrees (`flow_parity_break`); only a rest of 2 s or more on that hand resets it, since a full beat at 180 BPM is 0.33 s. Cuts 0.3 s or less apart reverse by at least 135 degrees (`fast_direction_break`). A dot counts as the reversal of the swing before it. A time you add or remove inside a phrase changes the parity of that hand's later cuts; leave those cuts to the placer, which re-chooses them when the edit breaks the flow. Sight lines were set 2026-09-23 after End of You 0:24: a note trails the note in front of it in its cell by 0.35 s in the centre middle/top cells and by 0.2 s elsewhere. One hand never streams three fast cuts alone (`one_hand_burst`). When pinned choices make a rule impossible, save refuses with a `placement_infeasible` error that names the beat, the notes, the rule and verified alternatives (unpin a field, or drop a time). Resolve it musically. Run `python -m sabermapper project check ID --workspace workspace --arrangement DRAFT.json` to see every finding a save would raise, with suggested edits, before saving; nothing is written. For standalone arrangements, `python -m sabermapper validate ARRANGEMENT.json` and `compile ARRANGEMENT.json --output NEW_FILE.dat` place the notes the same way. After saving a resolved project, export with `python -m sabermapper project export ID --workspace workspace`; the ZIP holds every difficulty of the project. For standalone arrangements, use `python -m sabermapper export ARRANGEMENT.json --audio TRACK.ogg --cover COVER.png --output NEW_MAP.zip`. Check audio identity, duration and cover validity; human review is not required to produce a provisional local ZIP. Standalone output files are created exclusively; choose a new name for each iteration. A clean validator is a structural check, not a playability judgment. Record authored, validated, compiled, exported and playtested status separately.

## Lightshow

Lights are generated from the same evidence as the notes and saved with the arrangement: `project save` creates or rebuilds them automatically, and export writes them. Read [lightshow](references/lightshow.md). After the notes are final, run `python -m sabermapper project lights-inspect ID --workspace workspace --start A --end B` on the drops, builds, breaks and the song's signature moments, and resolve the lighting warnings in `project check`. Improve the show through section overrides (`lightshow.sections`: mood, primary color, boost, white accents, intensity) and cues (`lightshow.cues`: pulse, off, spin, zoom, laser_speed, boost, event, clear), each tied to a sound you can name. Never edit `lightshow.generated`. `light_strobe` blocks save; keep full-field white flashes rare. `python -m sabermapper project lights ID --workspace workspace --revision REV` rebuilds the lights on demand, for example after setting `auto: false`.

Report exactly which checks were run (project check, audio grounding, lighting, export) and what placement chose (`placement` in the save result). Do not open ArcViewer, the studio or a browser preview unless the user asks: the agent cannot see the rendered view, and the user reviews saved revisions on their own schedule. Finish with the saved revision ID and export status, with remaining human timing and VR review clearly identified; do not stop the authorized local work merely because the user has not played it. When player feedback becomes available, save the exact verdict and use it for a scoped revision. Never claim listening, human approval or a game playtest from signal analysis, a synthetic result or local preview playback.
